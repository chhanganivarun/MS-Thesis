We investigate the Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) problem in the context of autonomous driving in outdoor settings. Existing VLN methods typically pose navigation as a node-selection problem on a discrete connected graph of the environment, or as grounding on a single static image. Both assumptions break down in dynamically changing outdoor scenes: a discrete map is often unavailable, and the region referred to by a language command may not yet be visible, may be occluded, or may leave the frame as the vehicle moves.

This thesis addresses these limitations by explicitly grounding the navigable regions corresponding to a textual command. At each timestamp, the model predicts a segmentation mask for the intermediate or final navigable region. The approach moves toward continuity in action space, provides interpretability through visual feedback, and supports finer manoeuvres such as ``park between the two cars''.

To enable training and live evaluation, we introduce CARLA-NAV, a novel meta-dataset built on the CARLA simulator. It comprises pre-recorded training sequences and a live environment for validation and testing, spanning multiple maps, weather conditions, and dynamic traffic. We further propose a multi-task network that jointly predicts navigable-region and short-term future-trajectory masks from multi-frame visual features, a historical contextual trajectory, and the language command, using a CLIP backbone. We also document earlier ViT and Conv3D (X3D) architectures evaluated with displacement metrics and stratified command ablations.

Experiments on CARLA-NAV (25 validation / 34 test episodes) show that combining multiple frames with historical trajectory context improves human-evaluated Task Completion, Fr\'{e}chet Distance, and normalized Dynamic Time Warping over single-image and non-contextual baselines, with Wilson confidence intervals reported for Task Completion. We also provide a formal analysis of grounding-first control and an extended evaluation protocol (map-unseen splits, safety infractions, trajectory-head ablation, and runtime). Relative to discrete-action outdoor VLN, the contribution is continuous-control region grounding with live episode evaluation in dynamic simulation.
