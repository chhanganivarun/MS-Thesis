This chapter presents our approach to Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) for autonomous driving in dynamic outdoor environments.\footnote{This chapter is based on Jain et al., \textit{Ground then Navigate: Language-guided Navigation in Dynamic Scenes}, IEEE ICRA 2023~\cite{jain2023ground}.} Building on the CARLA-NAV dataset introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:dataset}, we formalize the problem, describe a multi-task grounding network, explain live navigation, and report quantitative and qualitative results. Dataset creation details are not repeated here; we focus on modelling, inference, and evaluation.

\section{Background and Related Work}

Visual grounding associates linguistic descriptions with visual counterparts via proposal-based Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)~\cite{rohrbach2016grounding,hu2016natural,plummer2018conditional,rufus2020cosine,deng2018visual,zhang2018grounding,yang2019dynamic,qiu2020language} or pixel-level Referring Image Segmentation (RIS)~\cite{shi2018key,ye2019cross,huang2020referring,jain2021comprehensive}. Referring Navigable Regions (RNR)~\cite{Rufus_2021} localizes navigable road regions for language commands, but is limited to static images in pre-recorded sequences~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes}.

Indoor VLN has benefited from interactive environments~\cite{kolve2017ai2,wu2018building,chang2017matterport3d} and methods based on imitation learning~\cite{nguyen2019vision,nguyen2019help}, behaviour cloning~\cite{das2018neural}, sequence-to-sequence translation~\cite{anderson2018vision}, and cross-modal attention~\cite{cornia2019perceive}. Many approaches assume known topologies and discrete navigation graphs; Krantz et al.~\cite{krantz2020beyond} argue that nav-graph assumptions can inflate performance, and Hu et al.~\cite{hu2019you} question the role of vision when route structure alone can succeed in unseen environments.

Outdoor VLN on Google Street View and Touchdown~\cite{Chen2019TOUCHDOWN,mirowski2018learning,schumann2020generating} typically uses discrete actions~\cite{schumann-riezler-2022-analyzing,zhu-etal-2021-multimodal,xiang-etal-2020-learning}. CARLA-based language navigation has been limited in action diversity~\cite{sriram2019talk}, and Talk2Car focuses on object localization~\cite{deruyttere2019talk2car}. We instead reformulate RNR for dynamic outdoor settings and perform live navigation with continuous control via a local planner.

\section{Problem Statement}

Given video frames $V=\{v_{t-k}, v_{t-k+1}, \ldots, v_{t}\}$, a contextual historical trajectory $P$, and a language command $L = \{l_1, l_2, \ldots, l_N\}$, where $t$ is the current timestamp, $k$ is the historical window size, and $N$ is the maximum number of words, the goal is to predict the navigable mask $y_t$ and the future trajectory mask $z_t$ for the current frame $v_t$.

The contextual trajectory $P$ provides information about which part of a multi-step command has already been executed. For example, for ``turn left and park near the blue dustbin,'' $P$ indicates whether the left turn has already been taken. The spatial location of the navigation mask should determine the trajectory path's direction, and vice versa.

\section{Methodology}

We propose a multi-task network for navigation-region prediction and future-trajectory prediction. Both tasks are treated as dense prediction tasks for interpretability. During live inference, dense pixel points are converted to 3D world coordinates using inverse projective transformation. The architecture is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:proposed_pipeline}.

\subsection{Feature Extraction}

We utilize CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} to extract linguistic and visual features. For the linguistic expression $L$, we tokenize the command with the CLIP tokenizer and obtain word-level features $F^l = \{f^{l}_{1}, \ldots, f^{l}_{N}\}$ of shape $\mathbb{R}^{N \times C_l}$. For visual frames $V$, CLIP encodes video features $F^v=\{f^{v}_{t-k}, \ldots, f^{v}_{t}\}$. For trajectory context $P$, we project the past trajectory onto an image of the same size as $v_t$ and pass it through convolution and MLP layers to obtain $F^p$ with the same feature size as the per-frame visual features.

\subsection{Multi-Modal Fusion}

The trajectory context feature $F^p \in \mathbb{R}^{C_v \times 1 \times HW}$ is concatenated with video features $F^v$ along the temporal dimension, yielding $F^{vp} \in \mathbb{R}^{C_v \times (T+1) \times HW}$. We then apply multi-head self-attention over the joint contextual feature $F^{vp}$ and linguistic feature $F^l$:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:vln_attention}
\begin{split}
    F &= F^{vp} \odot F^l \\
    A &= \mathrm{Mhead}(F, F, F)\\
    M &= \mathrm{Conv3D}(A \ast F)
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes length-wise concatenation of word-level linguistic features with the joint visual--trajectory feature, $\mathrm{Mhead}$ is multi-head self-attention, $\ast$ denotes matrix multiplication, and $\mathrm{Conv3D}$ collapses the temporal dimension. $M$ is the final multi-modal contextual feature.

\subsection{Segmentation Heads and Loss}

Separate segmentation heads (sequences of convolutions with upsampling) predict the navigation and trajectory masks from $M$. We train with combo loss~\cite{taghanaki2019combo}, a combination of binary cross-entropy and Dice loss:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:combo_loss}
\begin{split}
    L_{\mathrm{bce}} &= -{(y_{t}\log(\hat{y_{t}}) + (1 - y_{t})\log(1 - \hat{y_{t}}))}\\
    L_{\mathrm{dice}} &= 2 \cdot \frac{\hat{y_{t}} \cap y_{t}}{\Sigma{\hat{y_{t}}} + \Sigma{y_{t}}}\\
    L_{\mathrm{combo}} &= \lambda L_{\mathrm{bce}} - (1 - \lambda) L_{\mathrm{dice}}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda = 0.3$. The approach is end-to-end trainable; the predicted trajectory correlates with the predicted navigation mask and indicates the vehicle's short-term route.

\subsection{Live Navigation}

To use the predicted navigable-region mask for navigation, we take the largest connected component and use its centroid as the target point for the local planner. As the vehicle approaches the final region, the predicted mask area tends to increase due to perspective. If the mask area exceeds an empirically chosen threshold for five consecutive predictions, we treat the region as the final goal and stop. During inference we predict every 5 frames; training uses every frame.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}

Following prior VLN work~\cite{schumann-riezler-2022-analyzing,xiang-etal-2020-learning,Chen2019TOUCHDOWN}, we use \textbf{Task Completion (TC)} as the primary success metric: whether the agent successfully navigates to the goal location for a given language command. We additionally use \textbf{Fr\'{e}chet Distance}~\cite{eifer1994frechet,eiter1994computing} and \textbf{normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW)}~\cite{ilharco2019general} to compare the live predicted path with the ground-truth path.

Fr\'{e}chet Distance and nDTW provide useful path-similarity signals, but they are not fully robust indicators of average navigation quality: a single early wrong turn can dominate the score, and a linguistic command may admit multiple valid trajectories (e.g., ``park near the blue dustbin''). Task Completion therefore remains the primary metric.

\section{Experimental Results}

\subsection{Implementation Details}

We use a CLIP backbone~\cite{radford2021learning} for feature extraction. Frames are selected with stride 10 and resized to $224 \times 224$. Per-frame visual features have spatial resolution $H=W=7$ and channel dimension $C_v=512$. The historical contextual trajectory is plotted from episode start to the current timestamp, resized to $640 \times 480$, and encoded to match the visual feature resolution. Linguistic features use the CLIP tokenizer and language encoder with maximum command length $N=20$ and $C_l=512$. We train with batch size 32 and AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2017decoupled}, initial learning rate $10^{-4}$, and polynomial learning-rate decay with power $0.5$. For combo loss, $\lambda = 0.3$. All methods and baselines were trained from scratch on CARLA-NAV.

\subsection{Baselines and Ablations}

We compare our proposed approach CLIP-MC (multiple frames with contextual trajectory) against:
\begin{itemize}
    \item RNR-S / RNR-SC: the RNR approach of Rufus et al.~\cite{Rufus_2021} with and without contextual trajectory, trained from scratch on CARLA-NAV;
    \item CLIP-S / CLIP-SC: single-frame CLIP variants without and with contextual trajectory;
    \item CLIP-M: multiple frames without contextual trajectory;
    \item CLIP-MC: multiple frames with contextual trajectory (proposed).
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{table:task_completion} reports Task Completion. CLIP-MC outperforms all variants. Introducing historical contextual trajectory improves validation TC by 4 percentage points for single-frame approaches (CLIP-SC $0.52$ vs.\ CLIP-S $0.48$) and by 16 percentage points for multi-frame approaches (CLIP-MC $0.72$ vs.\ CLIP-M $0.56$). The multi-frame approach CLIP-M improves by 8 percentage points over CLIP-S on both validation and test splits ($0.56$ / $0.55$ vs.\ $0.48$ / $0.47$). These results indicate that multiple frames and contextual trajectory are both required for effective outdoor VLN. CLIP-based single-frame models also outperform non-CLIP RNR variants on the test split, highlighting the benefit of CLIP multi-modal features.

\section{Failure Modes}

Qualitative inspection of live episodes, including the cases shown in Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_1}, reveals recurring failure patterns:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Delayed lane change:} On commands such as ``change to the left lane,'' CLIP-MC eventually executes the manoeuvre but with a slight delay relative to the ground-truth path, while RNR-S may continue straight. Temporal context helps, but reaction lag remains.
    \item \textbf{Curve mistaken for an intersection:} On a curved road approaching a traffic light, both CLIP-MC and RNR-S stop prematurely, treating the geometry as an intersection. This suggests sensitivity to road layout cues that resemble junction structure.
    \item \textbf{Early stopping from the area threshold:} When several candidate goal regions are visible, the area-based stopping rule can fire before the intended final region is reached, because a large connected component satisfies the threshold for five consecutive predictions.
    \item \textbf{Referential ambiguity and path metrics:} Commands such as ``park near the blue dustbin'' admit multiple valid stopping locations. Fr\'{e}chet Distance and nDTW then penalise a spatially plausible path that differs from the recorded demonstration, even when Task Completion may still succeed.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic-to-real domain gap:} All training and evaluation are in CARLA. Appearance, sensor noise, and traffic behaviour in the real world differ, so transfer without domain adaptation~\cite{kundu2021generalize,kang2020pixel} is an open risk.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}

Historical contextual trajectory informs the model which sub-instructions have already been executed, yielding large gains for multi-frame models. Multiple frames capture broader temporal visual context needed in dynamic scenes. CLIP provides stronger multi-modal features than a ResNet-style RNR backbone for aligning language with road imagery.

The area-based stopping criterion is simple and effective in many cases, but can be suboptimal when multiple candidate goal regions exist. Future improvements include confidence-aware stopping, proximity to the goal, temporal consistency of the mask, and semantic confirmation of referred objects~\cite{xiang-etal-2020-learning}. Domain adaptation toward real-world imagery~\cite{kundu2021generalize,kang2020pixel} is another important direction.

\subsection{Limitations of the Current Evaluation}

Several analyses that would strengthen the empirical story are not reported in this thesis, because they were not part of the original ICRA evaluation protocol:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No per-manoeuvre breakdown:} CARLA-NAV commands contain up to three sub-commands spanning turning, going straight, stopping, and parking, yet Task Completion and path metrics are reported only in aggregate.
    \item \textbf{Unevaluated trajectory head:} Future-trajectory prediction is used for interpretability and is never scored quantitatively against ground truth.
    \item \textbf{No runtime profiling:} Live navigation is demonstrated in CARLA, but wall-clock inference latency and frame rate are not reported, so the real-time claim is not numerically substantiated.
\end{itemize}

Stating these gaps explicitly is preferable to implying that the current metrics fully characterise system behaviour. They are natural targets for follow-up experiments.

\section{Chapter Summary}

We reformulated RNR for dynamic outdoor VLN, introduced a multi-task grounding network with CLIP features, multi-frame context, and historical trajectory, and demonstrated live navigation in CARLA. Quantitative results show that CLIP-MC substantially outperforms single-frame and non-contextual baselines on Task Completion, Fr\'{e}chet Distance, and nDTW. Failure modes and evaluation limitations clarify where the approach still falls short. The next chapter concludes the thesis and outlines future work.
